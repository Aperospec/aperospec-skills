# Editorial Copy Protocol

Read this reference whenever ConceptDeck must create or revise titles, page-ready language, voice, or companion copy.

## Responsibility

ConceptDeck owns the words the audience will actually read. It converts an approved narrative and evidence boundary into clear, specific, rhythmic language before VisualDirector begins composition.

It does not choose fonts, draw layouts, generate images, execute crops, repair overflow, or export files. VisualDirector may determine hierarchy and line breaks but may not silently rewrite the copy. Rendering may use an approved short variant but may not delete a limitation or alter meaning.

## 1. Find the Human Entry Point

Translate abstract capability into a perceptible human state. Prefer:

- a concrete person or familiar object;
- an ordinary action;
- a visible change or consequence;
- a desire, tension, surprise, risk, or decision the audience can feel.

Use technical terms only when they carry necessary meaning. Do not lead with taxonomy when a lived state can carry the same truth.

Example:

- Abstract: `single-image Three.js reconstruction`
- Human entry: `让桌上最珍爱的手办，住进电脑里`

This is a translation of focus, not permission to exaggerate capability.

## 2. Develop and Select the Editorial Angle

When the angle is not already locked, explore genuinely different cognitive triggers rather than synonym variations. Useful directions include:

- desired transformation;
- familiar frustration or loss;
- surprising contrast;
- concrete utility;
- consequence or tradeoff;
- a question the content can actually answer.

Select one angle using four checks:

1. It enters the audience's life quickly.
2. It fits the real subject rather than borrowing generic excitement.
3. The deck can fulfill its promise.
4. It stays inside the Evidence Lock.

Do not use false scarcity, unsupported numbers, absolute claims, generalized anxiety, or a question the content never resolves.

## 3. Build the Title System

Distinguish these roles:

- `Platform / document title`: discoverability and full proposition.
- `Cover display line`: the fastest readable promise.
- `Supporting deck line`: optional clarification, not a second headline.
- `Page title`: the current page's advancement.

Write the `Title Promise` as a plain statement of what the audience expects to receive. Record where the sequence pays it off. If the payoff does not exist, revise the title or the sequence before visual work begins.

## 4. Establish the Voice Contract

Define only the attributes that change the writing:

- audience;
- speaking distance;
- degree of orality;
- allowed technical register;
- emotional temperature;
- sentence length and rhythm;
- exact terms that must remain;
- prohibited voice or mannerisms.

When the user provides a writing reference, infer attributes rather than copying signature phrases, metaphors, or paragraph structures.

Avoid default AI prose: empty grandeur, symmetrical filler, repetitive conclusions, unexplained abstractions, fake intimacy, and decorative English labels.

## 5. Write Page-Ready Copy

Each page needs one editorial function and one real advancement. Choose the function from the content rather than forcing a fixed page count.

Possible functions include:

- enter through a desire, tension, or contrast;
- orient the audience to what changed;
- explain mechanism or causality;
- prove with evidence or observation;
- place the change in ordinary use;
- expose cost, limitation, or uncertainty;
- close with judgment, consequence, or next action.

For every page, deliver:

- `Display Title`: final visible title;
- `Short Title Variant`: pre-approved compression for tight space;
- `Narrative Sentence`: the sentence that advances cognition or emotion;
- `Supporting Copy`: only necessary explanation, evidence, or qualification;
- `Caption / Source Line`: attribution for evidence-bearing material;
- `Required Limitation`: a caveat that must remain visible;
- `Do Not Compress`: wording or meaning that layout cannot remove.

Do not write placeholders. Do not add microcopy solely because a composition has empty space.

## 6. Shape Language Across the Sequence

The copy must breathe with the narrative:

- high-impact pages use fewer, more concrete words;
- explanatory pages carry only the reasoning needed for the next step;
- evidence pages let evidence do visible work;
- pauses are allowed and should not be filled with slogans;
- limitations appear where the promise is evaluated, not buried at the end;
- closing language should add judgment or consequence, not repeat the cover.

Consecutive pages must not restate the same claim in different words.

## 7. Preserve Epistemic Boundaries

Tag the important statement on each page as fact, sourced opinion, inference, user judgment, imagination, or a clearly separated mix.

- Preserve exact names and approved wording from the Evidence Lock.
- Keep uncertainty proportional to the source.
- Do not turn a demonstration into a universal capability.
- Do not turn a future image into a current fact.
- Do not turn someone else's experience into the user's first-person claim.

If the approved inputs cannot support the intended sentence, send the issue back to Runtime. Do not solve an evidence gap with better prose.

## 8. Adapt Without Becoming a Channel Template

Editorial Copy is a general ConceptDeck capability. An active channel profile may change its shape:

- `Presentation`: titles support spoken argument; body copy does not compete with the speaker.
- `Concept / Exhibition Deck`: language may hold atmosphere and ambiguity, but must still carry meaning.
- `Xiaohongshu`: each card is understandable on its own; the cover enters daily life; the sequence and companion post fulfill the promise.
- `WeChat cover`: long and square variants are separately authored when required, not mechanically cropped from one line.

Channel conventions may compress language. They do not decide the project's cognitive or narrative direction.

## 9. Editorial QA and Rework Routing

Before handing the CDP to VisualDirector, verify:

- a real person, object, action, consequence, or felt state replaces unnecessary abstraction;
- the title and cover promise are fulfilled;
- every page advances the sequence;
- the Voice Contract is consistent without becoming monotonous;
- facts, opinion, inference, and imagination remain distinguishable;
- no important limitation is hidden or weakened;
- exact names and locks survive;
- no filler label, pseudo-magazine metadata, or empty slogan remains;
- the complete copy can be composed without downstream invention.

Route the first failure to its owner:

- missing or incorrect fact, source, limitation, or rights state -> Runtime / Injection;
- weak focus or no human relevance -> Project;
- broken emotional progression -> Cinema when active;
- weak angle, title, voice, payoff, page order, or wording -> ConceptDeck;
- visual hierarchy, line break, typography, or composition -> VisualDirector;
- crop execution, overflow, resolution, or export -> Rendering.
