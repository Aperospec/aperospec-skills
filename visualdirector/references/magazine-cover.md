# Editorial Cover and First-Frame Protocol

Use this reference for magazine-like covers, social thumbnails, title cards, document covers, and any first frame whose primary job is to earn the next look.

The goal is editorial hierarchy, not imitation of a printed magazine.

## 1. Define the Cover's Job

A cover must answer:

- who or what is this from, if identity is already meaningful?
- why is this particular piece worth opening?
- what relationship or promise must survive at small size?

Map printed-cover roles carefully:

- `Masthead` -> an approved account or stable series identifier; omit when no real identity system exists.
- `Lead title` -> the single primary promise.
- `Deck` -> at most one clarifying or tension-bearing line when needed.
- `Supporting lines` -> normally omit on mobile-first covers.
- `Issue / date / price / barcode` -> use only when factually and functionally real, never as decoration.

## 2. Protect the Image Relationship

Create a saliency and protection map before placing type:

- primary subject and gaze;
- relationship between subjects or worlds;
- brightest and most contrast-heavy regions;
- low-information zones;
- contours that type may follow or partially overlap;
- regions where type would destroy narrative meaning.

Text need not occupy a separate title box. It may align with a frame, follow a contour, enter depth, or use a quiet region, but image and type must create one reading loop.

## 3. Use a Real Information Budget

Default mobile-first hierarchy:

1. primary visual relationship;
2. one lead title;
3. one optional deck.

Delete any label that does not change understanding, identity, proof, or navigation. Empty space is not a reason to add copy.

## 4. Choose Typeface Personality

Select type by semantic fit:

- human, domestic, intimate, playful, technical, documentary, urgent, institutional, or contemplative;
- weight and width that suit viewing distance;
- one coherent family before adding another;
- color taken from the visual system rather than a decorative palette.

Do not use a prestige serif, oversized black type, or editorial ornament merely to signal “magazine.”

For Chinese titles, specify semantic line breaks. Keep phrases, verb-object relationships, names, numbers, and negation intact. Rendering may not break lines only to satisfy a container.

## 5. Prove at Small Size Before Final Art

For a new, subjective, or brand-facing cover direction, prepare up to three materially different small composition proofs. Each proof must change the image-type relationship or hierarchy, not just color or alignment.

VisualDirector recommends one. Runtime asks the human to choose only when the alternatives would materially change the cover's emotional stance or identity. After approval, lock the selected relationship before high-resolution production.

Test at the real viewing condition:

- primary relationship is readable without zoom;
- lead title is readable quickly;
- deck does not compete with the lead;
- subject is not damaged by crop or platform overlay;
- contrast survives the reduced size;
- the cover still works with any nonessential microcopy removed.

For Xiaohongshu, use approximately 360 px width as the early proof and the active channel profile's current dimensions for final export.

## 6. Renderer Handoff

The VDP must specify:

- approved proof and hierarchy;
- semantic line breaks;
- protected subject and allowed crop regions;
- title and deck safe zones;
- approved short variant;
- exact deterministic copy source;
- small-size pass condition;
- changes that require return to VisualDirector or ConceptDeck.

Generate or select the textless visual first. Compose exact Chinese deterministically after the visual direction is locked.

## 7. Cover Failure Routing

- wrong promise or too much copy -> ConceptDeck;
- wrong emotional stance, hierarchy, image-type loop, or type personality -> VisualDirector;
- wrong crop coordinates, overflow, font loading, contrast execution, or export -> Rendering;
- copied reference subject, layout, or unlicensed asset -> Runtime / Injection.

Fix one failed layer at a time. Do not rebuild the whole cover because one technical dimension failed.
