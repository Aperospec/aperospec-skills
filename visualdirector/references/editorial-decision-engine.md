# Editorial Visual Decision Engine

Use this reference for visual reading, Art Direction, editorial planning, preflight review, and targeted repair.

## 1. Read Before Designing

Do not begin with a template or a style label. First map what the content or approved image is already doing.

For an existing image, identify:

- subject, gaze, action, and relationship;
- foreground, middle ground, background, depth, and overlap;
- brightest, most colorful, highest-contrast, and most semantically important regions;
- narrative protection zones that type must not destroy;
- quiet zones that can carry type;
- light direction, color anchors, surface and material cues;
- what the viewer feels before reading any words.

For a visual that does not exist yet, create a planned scene map with the same fields. Do not claim pixel-level saliency analysis when there are no pixels.

Treat screenshots, diagrams, outputs, failures, and source evidence as potential primary visuals. A decorative Hero image is not automatically more important than proof.

## 2. Form Art Direction Before Layout

Translate the CDP into an Art Direction thesis that joins:

`content focus + felt state + subject relationship + visual behavior`

Internally consider genuinely different directions when the problem is open. Compare each direction on:

- fidelity to the page's cognitive and emotional task;
- fit with the audience's viewing state and output surface;
- originality inside the reference and rights boundary;
- ability to survive the final size;
- production feasibility and evidence needs;
- what it sacrifices.

Recommend one direction. Do not ask the human to choose among superficial variations. A Runtime Visual Direction Gate is justified only when alternatives would materially change meaning, emotional stance, brand identity, rights exposure, cost, or production scope.

If the choice is ordinary—camera height, prop count, exact alignment, element placement, or a font shortlist—VisualDirector decides it.

## 3. Build Editorial Hierarchy

Define what the audience should perceive at three depths:

1. first glance: the primary relationship or promise;
2. second glance: the lead title or decisive explanation;
3. third glance: support, evidence, source, or limitation.

Not every page needs all three. A pause may intentionally stop at one layer.

For each page decide:

- one primary task and one visual center;
- whether the page is image-led, evidence-led, diagram-led, type-led, or intentionally quiet;
- how title and image form one reading path;
- where the grid provides order and where one deliberate break adds energy;
- how negative space protects impact rather than merely filling unused canvas;
- what may disappear at thumbnail or distance view and what must survive.

Do not force every page into the same template. Preserve shared grammar through type personality, color relationships, margins, source syntax, image treatment, and rhythm—not identical composition.

## 4. Direct Typography Without Rewriting

Typography decisions include:

- personality and weight range;
- hierarchy and relative scale;
- line length and semantic Chinese line breaks;
- density, alignment, overlap, edge contact, and depth behavior;
- contrast against the actual image;
- relationship to the subject's gaze, contour, movement, and quiet zones.

Use only locked CDP copy. VisualDirector may select an approved short variant and specify semantic line breaks. It may not invent a label, slogan, English campaign phrase, number, category, or pseudo-editorial metadata.

If the copy cannot fit without changing meaning, return to ConceptDeck. Do not solve the problem with tiny type or hidden limitations.

## 5. Plan Assets and Evidence

Assign every asset a role:

- enter: establish an emotional or lived state;
- explain: reveal mechanism or structure;
- prove: carry screenshot, output, test, source, or comparison evidence;
- limit: make uncertainty or failure visible;
- connect: preserve rhythm or transition;
- identify: carry a restrained, approved account or series marker.

Every generated asset should be textless unless VDP explicitly requires non-language texture or marks. Exact Chinese and factual labels are composed deterministically downstream.

Provide a fallback for any asset with material generation risk. A fallback must preserve the page task; it cannot replace a specific proof with generic decoration.

## 6. Apply References Without Copying

When Style DNA is supplied, extract only visual attributes such as:

- surface, material, edge, brush or rendering behavior;
- light, color relationships, grain, depth, and composition temperament;
- typography compatibility.

Do not transfer subjects, objects, characters, products, logos, exact layouts, poses, scenes, narrative events, or a branded style signature. If a reference cannot be used without recognizable imitation, reject it or reduce it to a more abstract attribute.

## 7. Preflight Before Rendering

Check the planned VDP for conflicts:

- does the visual promise match the CDP promise?
- can the primary relationship survive without text?
- does typography have a real quiet zone or intentional image interaction?
- are proof and limitations visually strong enough?
- are channel size, crop, safe area, and viewing distance addressed?
- can available assets and tools execute the direction?
- are reference, rights, and Brand Visual Lock boundaries explicit?
- does the renderer have a fallback that preserves meaning?

If a conflict requires a different Art Direction, stop before rendering. If it only requires execution detail, resolve it autonomously.

## 8. Review and Targeted Repair

Evaluate distinct dimensions separately:

- content fidelity;
- subject identity and relationship;
- composition and reading path;
- saliency and focal priority;
- color and light behavior;
- typography personality and legibility;
- image-type integration;
- style / brand consistency;
- evidence visibility;
- channel and small-size survival;
- reference and rights compliance.

Repair only the first failed owner:

- wrong fact or asset permission -> Runtime / Injection;
- wrong focus -> Project;
- broken emotional progression -> Cinema;
- wrong title, wording, or sequence -> ConceptDeck;
- wrong metaphor, composition, hierarchy, or typography personality -> VisualDirector;
- wrong crop coordinates, overflow, resolution, or export -> Rendering.

Continue only targeted repairs within Runtime's bounded attempt budget. Pause when the same failure persists, another validated dimension regresses, the proposed repair changes direction, or the available fallback lowers the promised quality.
