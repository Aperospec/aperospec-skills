# Aperospec Causal Trigger Patterns

## Full Reasoning Template

```markdown
## 1. Phenomenon
[What is visibly happening, being discussed, requested, feared, desired, or built?]

## 2. Past: backward tracing
[Where did this begin? What historical, social, environmental, psychological, technological, or civilizational structure produced it?]

## 3. Root cause
[What deeper drive force actually makes this phenomenon operate?]

## 4. Present validation
[How has the root cause evolved into the current state? What evidence shows that the drive force is active now?]

## 5. Focus
[What issue, tension, or visible question has the causal chain pushed into view now?]

## 6. Future projection
[If the drive force continues, what future image, behavior, emotion, social relationship, or system condition may appear?]

## 7. Drive-force map
[What is truly driving people or systems: fear, desire, identity, risk, survival pressure, environment, interest, social structure, technology, or civilizational inertia?]

## 8. Cognitive trigger
[What can pierce surface cognition and make people feel, react, act, or rethink?]

## 9. Perceptible translation
[What situation, state, world, role, scene, or experience can make the abstract structure felt?]

## 10. Immersive cognition
[How do people enter this state, experience emotional change, and form reflection?]
```

## Three-Layer Check

Use this to avoid staying on the surface.

```markdown
Surface phenomenon:
[What appears to be happening.]

Surface rule:
[How the system explains, legitimizes, or describes itself.]

Deep drive:
[The real force making the system move.]
```

## Focus Check

`焦点` is not the background.

`焦点` is not the root cause.

`焦点` is:

> the current issue or visible tension pushed forward by the background and causal chain.

Test a focus with:
- Does it reveal a real contradiction?
- Can it influence future direction?
- Does it affect emotion or behavior?
- Can it trigger attention or response?
- Is it specific enough to be experienced, not just discussed?

## Drive-Force Categories

Look for real forces such as:
- interest
- risk
- fear
- desire
- emotion
- environment
- identity
- survival pressure
- social structure
- technological change
- civilizational inertia

Avoid treating slogans, official explanations, or formal values as the real driver unless they demonstrably change behavior.

## Cognitive Trigger Questions

After finding the drive force, ask:
- What will make people emotionally react?
- What will make people act differently?
- What will break the illusion of surface understanding?
- What will make a system respond?
- What state must people enter before they understand?

## Translation Rule

Abstract structures should become perceptible states.

Examples:
- pressure becomes a narrowing path
- ecological collapse becomes future ruins
- identity anxiety becomes a role-seeking world
- technological intrusion becomes a sacred atmosphere broken by screens
- social temptation becomes a friendly invitation in a risky environment

## Final Compression

A complete Aperospec judgment can be compressed as:

```markdown
This phenomenon is not merely [surface event].
It is the result of [root drive] evolving over time.
Today, it has pushed [focus] into view.
If the drive continues, it may lead to [future image].
The real cognitive trigger is [trigger].
Therefore, it should be translated into [perceptible state],
so people can enter, feel, react, and reflect.
```
