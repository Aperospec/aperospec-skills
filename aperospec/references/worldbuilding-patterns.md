# Aperospec Worldbuilding Patterns

## First-Principles Analysis Template

```markdown
## 1. Phenomenon
[What is visibly being discussed, requested, built, feared, desired, or displayed?]

## 2. Why Now
[Why does this topic appear in this era? What pressure, trend, event, anxiety, opportunity, or contradiction activates it?]

## 3. Stimulus Point
[The active trigger: incident, policy, technology shift, social mood, crisis, commercial pressure, cultural contradiction, or hidden desire.]

## 4. Root Cause
[What deeper environmental, social, psychological, civilizational, or human cause produces the phenomenon?]

## 5. Core Contradiction
[The central tension that the experience must make felt.]

## 6. Design Position
[What stance does the user take as Narrative Worldbuilder / Experience Director / Civilization Narrative Designer?]
```

## Root-Cause Ladder

Use this pattern to avoid staying at the surface.

```markdown
Surface topic:
[What the project appears to be about.]

First why:
[Why is this topic needed?]

Second why:
[What environment produces this need?]

Third why:
[What social, emotional, civilizational, or human pattern sits underneath?]

Real design object:
[The thing that must actually be constructed, protected, exposed, healed, or questioned.]
```

## Worldview Translation Template

```markdown
Essence:
[The root problem in one sentence.]

Enterable world:
[A concrete world that lets people feel the essence: place, time, atmosphere, rules, people, materials, and tension.]

Core image:
[One image that can represent the whole worldview before explanation.]

World rules:
- [What force shapes this world?]
- [What behavior is rewarded or punished?]
- [What is disguised as ordinary life?]
- [What future consequence is approaching?]

Narrative promise:
[What emotional and intellectual journey will the audience go through?]
```

## Emotional Curve Template

Use this before defining any output medium.

```markdown
1. Enter: [First atmosphere and threshold]
2. Attract: [What pulls the audience in]
3. Deepen: [What hidden mechanism is revealed]
4. Unease: [What begins to feel wrong]
5. Pressure: [How the world closes in]
6. Collapse: [What consequence becomes unavoidable]
7. Reflection: [What the audience must reconsider]
8. Reconstruction: [What belief, action, identity, or future image remains]
```

## Identity Immersion Template

```markdown
Audience role:
[Who does the audience become?]

Entry ritual:
[How the audience receives identity: token, wristband, invitation, oath, test, guide, faction, mission, etc.]

Role progression:
[How the audience's identity changes through the journey.]

Choice or consequence:
[What decision, data result, emotional realization, or narrative consequence makes the role meaningful?]

Exit transformation:
[What does the audience take away as a changed identity?]
```

## Civilization And Technology Judgment

Before adding technology, ask:
- Does it preserve or destroy the world's temperament?
- Does it serve emotion, identity, ritual, memory, or knowledge?
- Could a quieter method be stronger?
- Is the technology hidden behind civilization, or is it performing itself?
- Would the concept still stand without this technology?

Use restrained technology when the topic involves ritual, sacredness, historical continuity, cultural memory, or civilizational sediment.

## Upstream Critique Checklist

- Does the thinking begin from phenomenon and why-now rather than output format?
- Has the root cause moved beyond slogans?
- Is the core contradiction sharp enough to direct decisions?
- Is there an enterable world, not just a theme?
- Does the emotional curve come before knowledge arrangement?
- Does identity immersion prevent passive viewing?
- Does the technology stance protect the world's temperament?
- Does the final position reveal how the user understands the world?
